## 6. WNODE RULESET (Summarized)
*   **1 Soul = 1 Vote**: Governance power is strictly tied to verified human identity; tokens and capital cannot buy governance influence [18, 19].
*   **Soul Permanence**: A Soul cannot be sold, traded, or transferred, and is bound permanently to a single Wnode Universal Identifier (WUID) [20, 21].
*   **Separation of Powers**: Commercial assets (like Tokens and Affiliate Trees) grant zero governance power [22, 23].
*   **100% Flow-Through Economics**: The DAO retains zero profit [24, 25]. Every fiat dollar entering the mesh is distributed as follows: 70% to Node Operators, 10% to Sales Source, 10% to Affiliate Tree (3% Level 1, 7% Level 2), 7% to the Steward, and 3% to the Founder/Infrastructure Manager [26, 27].
*   **Zero-Trust Execution**: Nodes cannot access user data, keys, or job history; all execution must be ephemeral and restricted to RAM [28, 29].

## 7. Governance Model
Wnode utilizes a hybrid constitutional system designed to prevent whale dominance and centralisation [30, 31].
*   **The DAO (Wnode DAO)**: The sovereign community body formed by verified human operators (Voting Souls) that votes on proposals, treasury spending, and amendments [30, 32].
*   **Founder Board**: A four-member body acting as constitutional guardians with emergency powers to protect the mesh from hostile capture; transitions partially to DAO-elected seats as the network matures [33-35].
*   **Governance Board**: The operational oversight layer that validates DAO proposals, supervises the treasury, and ensures decisions are implemented [36, 37].
*   **Steward**: A commercial operator holding a perpetual, revenue-bearing license to manage day-to-day UX, compliance, and customer support, but possessing no governance power [38, 39].
*   **Infrastructure Manager (IM)**: The independent technical authority responsible for network uptime, emergency response, node verification, and hardware integrity [40, 41].

## 8. Wnode Network Architecture
The network topology is self-balancing, fault-tolerant, and horizontally scalable [42]. 
*   **Execution Clients**: Includes WASM nodes that run securely in a web browser, and Native Node Operators (Go binaries) installed on desktop machines or servers for heavier workloads [43, 44].
*   **Distributed Routing Engine**: Dynamically accepts jobs, splits them into shards, and routes them to nodes based on locality, hardware capability, latency, and trust scoring [44, 45].
*   **Node Classes**: Ranges from Standard (consumer laptops), Edge (IoT/Mobile), Pro (high-performance rigs), to Enterprise (data center-grade clusters) [46].
*   **Verification Layer**: Compute outputs are cryptographically verified for correctness using redundant execution, deterministic replay, and hash matching [47].
